Core Theory

Chapter, Concept, and Exhibit: Definitions

1

Chapter

The curriculum unit for a class and subject, with aim, sessions, and chapter-level identity.

2

Concept

A teachable block inside a chapter that captures outcomes, methods, activities, and life connection.

3

Exhibit

Supporting artifact linked to a concept: rich text, image, audio, video, or curated link.

4

Learning Delivery

Teachers present concepts and exhibits in sequence to keep instruction focused and measurable.

Think of this as: Chapter = unit plan, Concept = lesson idea, Exhibit = evidence or learning aid.

Quality Characteristics

How to Judge Good Structure

Chapter Characteristics

Has clear title, class, subject, time estimate, and aim. Broad enough to frame a unit, specific enough to guide planning.

Concept Characteristics

One concept should teach one core idea. Outcomes must be observable, and activities should map to outcomes directly.

Exhibit Characteristics

Every exhibit should have purpose: explain, demonstrate, reinforce, or assess. Keep media age-appropriate and classroom-ready.

Quality check line: "If an exhibit does not strengthen concept understanding, it should not be added."

Inter-Relationships

How Chapter, Concepts, and Exhibits Connect

Hierarchy and Ownership

  • One chapter contains many concepts
  • One concept can contain many exhibits
  • Removing a chapter removes dependent concepts and exhibits
  • Reordering exhibits changes teaching sequence, not meaning

Pedagogy Mapping

  • Chapter aim should be achieved through concept outcomes
  • Concept activities should reference relevant exhibits
  • Exhibits should appear near the concept where they are used
  • Session planning should align with concept complexity

Model formula: Chapter Goal = Sum of Concept Outcomes; Concept Clarity = Content + Right Exhibits.

Public View

How Students and Teachers Consume Content

Navigation Path

  • Home selects class, then subject dashboard opens
  • Chapter list shows chapter scope and session estimate
  • Chapter detail reveals concept-by-concept structure
  • Exhibits open as focused learning support in modal

What Public View Communicates

  • Clear chapter intent before detailed content
  • Concept blocks that match classroom flow
  • Exhibits that reduce explanation time in class
  • Consistent structure across all subjects and classes

Live demo cue: show one complete chain from chapter aim to concept outcome to exhibit evidence.

Portal for Educators

How Teachers Edit and Maintain Content

Chapter Operations

Teachers can create, filter, and edit chapters by class and subject. Session totals and concept counts help track coverage.

Concept Authoring

Rich text editor supports lists, links, images, and tables. Teachers can express pedagogy clearly without writing HTML.

Exhibit Management

Educators can add rich text and media exhibits, then reorder them to match lesson flow. Output appears directly in public view.

Demo line: "Now we switch roles: same content model, but this interface is for teacher and educator creation with controlled updates."

Live Demo Script

Suggested Walkthrough (15 Minutes)

Part A: Theory to Public (7 min)

  • Explain chapter, concept, exhibit model using one real chapter
  • Open public side and follow class → subject → chapter
  • Point out where concept outcomes appear
  • Open an exhibit and explain its instructional purpose
  • Connect exhibit back to concept and chapter aim

Part B: Teacher Portal Editing (8 min)

  • Login to portal and open chapter edit
  • Edit one concept using rich text and structured sections
  • Add one exhibit with clear pedagogic intent
  • Reorder exhibits to improve teaching sequence
  • Save and verify immediately in public view
  • Close with roles and ownership expectations

Confidence close: "Model clarity plus easy editing leads to consistent classroom delivery."

Role Clarity

Who Does What After Training

Subject Teachers

Own concept quality, activity text, and exhibit relevance. Keep each concept instructional and measurable.

Academic Leads

Review chapter-level alignment: aim, sessions, and concept sequence. Ensure depth and progression are balanced.

Admins

Manage users, access, classes, and subjects. Support workflow but preserve academic ownership with teachers.

Operational tip: use a monthly review cycle where one chapter per subject is quality-audited.
